# Publication Readiness — BOI Project 1

## Recommended title
**Graph-Based Combinatorial Optimization for Agricultural Market Connectivity in Burundi: A Spatial and Transport-Cost Sensitivity Framework**

## Defensible contribution
An integrated analytical workflow combining agricultural market-price observations, graph-based road distances, transport-cost sensitivity, route robustness, corridor prioritization, and normalized multi-product allocation.

## Claims that are supported
- The framework screens market-pair price gaps against alternative transport-cost assumptions.
- Network distance is used instead of relying solely on straight-line distance for the transport analysis.
- Corridor priorities can be ranked using robustness, product breadth, margin and spatial indicators.
- The framework exposes how results change across transport-cost and stress scenarios.

## Claims to avoid
- Actual or realized agricultural shipment volumes.
- Observed freight tariffs at the corridor level.
- Causal claims that road connectivity caused price convergence/divergence.
- Investment returns or bankable infrastructure recommendations.
- Calibrated supply-demand equilibrium or optimal real-world shipment plans.

## Minimum empirical upgrades before journal submission
1. Collect corridor-level freight tariffs and trip costs.
2. Add market-level supply and demand quantities by product and season.
3. Add vehicle capacities and loading/handling/loss costs.
4. Validate road accessibility with seasonal condition and closure data.
5. Cross-check priority corridors with observed trade flows or field interviews.
6. Re-run the optimization with calibrated quantities and costs.
7. Report uncertainty and, where possible, confidence intervals or bootstrap stability.
