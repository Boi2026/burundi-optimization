# Phase 14 — External Validation Protocol

## 1. Research question
Do the priority corridors and transport-cost sensitivity results remain plausible when confronted with observed freight costs, market quantities, road accessibility and transaction prices?

## 2. Validation design
Use stratified corridor sampling:
- Tier A corridors: highest priority.
- Tier B corridors: secondary priority.
- Geographic coverage: distribute observations across Burundi's major regions.
- Product coverage: prioritize the ten modeled commodities.
- Direction: record both origin-to-destination direction and return direction where possible.
- Seasonality: record the observation date and seasonal conditions.

## 3. Minimum freight observation
Each observation should contain:
- origin market
- destination market
- commodity
- date
- vehicle type
- payload tonnes
- quoted/paid freight cost BIF
- trip distance if observed
- fuel price
- loading/unloading costs
- tolls/taxes/fees
- spoilage/loss
- source type
- respondent/source reference
- confidence/verification status

## 4. Supply and demand
Collect quantities by:
- market
- product
- month/season
- available supply
- observed demand or sales volume
- units and conversion factor
- source and provenance

## 5. Road accessibility
For sampled corridors record:
- road condition
- passability
- closure/delay
- seasonal constraints
- observed travel time
- date
- evidence/source

## 6. Price validation
Collect transaction-level or market-level prices with:
- exact date
- market
- product
- quality/grade where relevant
- unit
- price BIF
- source
- verification status

## 7. Cost decomposition
Where possible separate:
- freight
- loading
- unloading
- handling
- spoilage/loss
- taxes/fees
- informal charges
- other trip costs

## 8. Model recalibration
After quality control:
1. Convert freight costs to BIF/t-km.
2. Compare observations with 500–1,500 BIF/t-km.
3. Replace illustrative assumptions only where empirical observations are adequate.
4. Add observed supply/demand/capacity constraints.
5. Re-run optimization.
6. Re-run robustness and corridor ranking.
7. Report changes rather than forcing agreement with the existing model.

## 9. Validation standard
A result that contradicts the current model is not a failure. It is empirical information and must be reported transparently.
